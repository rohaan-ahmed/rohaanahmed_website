For a long time, I have thought about starting a personal blog, but the thought of being just another voice shouting into the aether with nobody listening has kept me from doing it. I don't even have a Twitter account for this reason - there's already way too much noise.

To add to this, due to natural career progression, in recent years I have found myself spending more time working inside Microsoft Word and Excel than inside VScode/ Cursor, or more time writing technical tradeoff sections than Python code or drawing electrical wiring diagrams. Therefore, on most days after I closed my work laptop, I did not really feel like writing more. 

However, things have changed in the last 12-16 months. Working in, and with, AI as much as I do on a daily basis (which is a ton of fun, by the way), I've found myself writing less and managing AI more. Most of the "writing" I do ends up being either prompts, summaries, or chat messages to my team directing them on tasks. I don't lament less time with Word, but I do miss the simple act of "writing". Thinking back to high-school, I remember that actually really enjoyed composition - despite always preferring Physics over all other subjects. I distinctly remember writing essays by hand in Grade 11 English class, which were occasionally selected by Ms. Hommel to read out to the rest of the class as "examples of good writing" (I guess I loved the attention). 

Therefore, since I have thoughts - lots of them, on many topics - and I miss writing, I've created this corner on the internet to share both. I have no expectations from you, reader, but I do have a promise to make for now.

## This will be a Non Generative AI Zone

Everything I write will be hand-typed, and reflect my thoughts - as original or un-original as they might be. I may, occasionally, use AI to argue against me to think ideas through, or to summarize my writing, but I will not use AI to write content, or to fix my writing, or to "make this sound more polished". No. The ideas, prose, and mistakes will be my own - even when it looks like they're not. I promise that I used to use the 3 example rule well before AI ruined it - maybe I learned it from Ms. Hommel.

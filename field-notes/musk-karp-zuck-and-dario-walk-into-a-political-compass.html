Musk, Karp, Zuck, and Dario Walk Into a Political Compass

I made an AI-specific political compass because “left” and “right” are useless labels for companies. For example, Musk and Karp are both right-wing while disagreeing on almost everything that actually matters in AI.

Read on Medium Aug 23, 2026

Disclaimer: No AI was used in the writing, editing, or reviewing of this article. I did, however, use GPT as a research assistant to find the posts and articles I vaguely remembered, and to generate the actual plot.

Note: I am typing this draft sitting in the Economy class of an Air Canada flight from Boston to Toronto — returning from a military training course. This factoid is completely unnecessary for the remainder of this article.

Let me be clear, I use an unhealthy amount of AI in my daily life — both at work and at home. I am pro data-centers (mostly), pro AI-development (mostly), and serve in the military as a Reservist — which would mark me, in most people’s minds, as a firm right-winger.

Except, I consider myself center-left (which is an embarrassing thing to admit these days, I concede). I am pro-immigration, pro-social welfare, pro-gun control, and pro-Palestine.

This makes me a walking contradiction — a tech bro who distrusts tech bros, a snowflake who likes rifles, a grunt who believes in social programs. In other words, you are probably already disappointed — and that’s the sweet spot where I live.

Maybe that’s why, but the traditional Left vs Right categorization of AI companies seems useless to me. Musk, Thiel, Karp, even the Zuck — are all right-wingers now. Dario is Left. Easy peasy. Job done. Right?

No. SpacexAI and Palantir demonstrate the problem. They both seem Right-wing, but their theories of institutional power are almost opposite.

Obviously, then, I am here to fix this all.

Disclaimer: This is based purely on my opinions as of 22 August 2026. It could change at any time, for any reason. Don’t email me because you think OpenAI should be 5 pixels to the right.

The above plot is based on my understanding of each company’s policies, actions, products, and CEO/Founder statements. I am not claiming that every employee at Google, for example, shares Demis Hassabis’ political philosophy.

The Horizontal Axis: Globalist / Universalist vs Techno-Nationalist / Sovereigntist

I asked myself the question: Who does this company think AI should serve?

On the left, we have the idea that AI should serve humanity, foster international cooperation, distribute benefits globally, and have international governance. As my friend Mohammad would say, Everybody Love Everybody.

On the right, we have those who believe in national technological leadership, sovereignty, and geopolitical edge. Companies that advocate for Wester / American / European strategic interests.

The Vertical Axis: Open / Libertarian vs Institutionalist / Managed Governance

The question I asked here was: Who does this company think should control AI?

At the top, we have a strong belief in safety frameworks, controlled deployment, regulation, oversight, and bureaucratic guardrails (“bureaucratic” here is not a derogatory term)

On the bottom: Open weights, broad access, fewer restrictions, decentralized control, and availability.

Anthropic: D̶o̶n̶’̶t̶ ̶b̶e̶ ̶e̶v̶i̶l̶ Please Don’t Kill Everyone

Easiest one. Upper Left. They have a “Responsible Scaling Policy” with capability thresholds, risk assessments, and explicit mechanisms for determining when a model is too dangerous (however that works).

Dario has spent years warning about advanced AI — you could say it is the raison d’être for Anthropic (yeah, I French sometimes) — whilst also leading Anthropic into its well-deserved Number 1 spot.

I am not the first to point out, but there is something inherently funny (if not untrustworthy) about building increasingly powerful AI while also warning against its proliferation.

“This machine could end civilization.”
“Anyway, Claude Saga 7 drops Tuesday. It will cost as much as your rent, and we will degrade performance if we think you are not worthy”

Still, the philosophy is consistent, even if Dario wants to rebrand pessimism into optimism. Anthropic thinks advanced AI could be enormously consequential for humanity and that serious institutions need to manage the risks.

Google DeepMind: Anthropic with Shareholders

Still globalist. Still highly institutional. Slightly closer to the center.

Demis Hassabis regularly describes AGI as transformative for humanity while arguing that we need to get there safely. DeepMind has the “Frontier Safety Framework” roughly doing the same thing as Anthropic’s policy.

The difference is that DeepMind is Google — one of the largest corporations in human history which is, in my opinion, extremely well placed within the AI race. DeepMind is Anthropic with much better corporate discipline, far worse models, and more diverse optionality.

Microsoft: Your AGI requires Administrator Permission

Speaking of v, here is the father of them all. Microsoft may be the most boringly institutional AI company in the world, which I mean as a compliment.

Microsoft has high institutionalism, but is relatively centrist geopolitically.

Responsible AI standards, enterprise governance, compliance, finely-tuned models, regulated industries, committees (so many committees), and — most importantly of all — the trust of the vast majority of the enterprise world.

Dario worries AGI might destroy humanity, Satya just wants to make sure it gets the right access permissions to do so.

OpenAI: Some Terms and Conditions Apply

OpenAI should not be a dot, it should be a line. Starting as a non-profit ensuring AI would “benefit all humanity” and open-sourcing or publishing everything, they are now one of the world’s most valuable, and closed, companies. Increasingly working with the US government and national security, offering to “gift” the administration equity.

Amazing what billions of dollars in potential revenue can do to a company’s Vision Statement.

Today I put OpenAI above the centre line and slightly toward the techno-nationalist side — but that could change by next Tuesday after Sam’s next meeting with Trump.

Meta: Masculine energy, lifting weights, and open weights

We are at the fourth ideological reboot of the Zuck. Swifties would call it the City Boy Era or something (I’m 36, I have no idea how this works).

  • First, it was the hoodie-clad hacker who wanted everyone to share everything.
  • Then came the CEO of content-moderation
  • Then came the metaverse, and I don’t even know what the heck all that was about.
  • Current Zuckerberg does MMA, talks about free expression, champions open AI, wants more “masculine energy” in corporate culture, and wants ‘Murica to lead in AI.

Amazing character arc.

Underneath the memes, we have a resurgent AI company. Llama, Spark, and Glimmer — they are playing in both the open and closed spaces. Zuck has proselytized that AI shouldn’t be concentrated in a handful of companies, yet believes in American AI dominance and overall testosterone levels.

Mistral: le Meta de l’Europe

It’s similar to Meta, but for France. Unless you’re French, you probably don’t care too much about their philosophy beyond this fact. I’m not French, so let’s move on.

Palantir: If we don’t build the atomic bomb, somebody else will, so it’s better if we do it

I was tempted to put them close to the libertarian end, mainly because of Peter Thiel who famously no longer believes that “freedom and democracy are compatible”. Rich coming from someone associated with selling intelligence software to governments.

Karp is, perhaps, the more relevant figure for Palantir’s current philosophy. In the “The Technological Republic” he essentially argues that Silicon Valley was focused too much on advertising, social media, and food delivery, when what they should have been doing was building Sparta — making America great ag… nevermind.

I partially agree, I don’t need seventeen ways of ordering shawarma in Toronto, but Karp is more militant than even I am (and I’m in the militia). He talks about Western power, military superiority, supporting the state… all things that would make somebody with a passing understanding of history quite nervous.

Palantir doesn’t want the West to be weak. Palantir wants the West to buy Palantir’s software.

Far-right on the sovereignty axis, above the institutional line.

SpacexAI: What is institutions are the problem?

Disclaimer, I don’t know what to call this company, so I’ll call it xAI.

Elon reminds me of my old university buddy who was liberal when he was drawing student loans to pay his tuition, but turned hard-right when he started earning $150K a year. He is also an immigrant who doesn’t like immigrants.

Musk believes many Western institutions are the problem — they are too woke, too dumb, too lazy. Grok is explicitly positioned as the non-woke, non-DEI, pro-free-speech alternative to “politically correct” and “mainstream” AI systems. Musk’s version of free speech is wonderfully simple: governments shouldn’t decide what you can see. Elon Musk should.

To be clear, xAI isn’t uncensored, we know this from the success the pro-Israeli lobby has had with Musk. Instead, it’s focused on telling you how everyone else is censoring you. xAI is currently challenging Minnesota’s AI “nudification” law on free-speech grounds, while simultaneously suing users who bypass its own safeguards. This contradiction is almost suspiciously perfect.

xAI’s worldview is, nevertheless, distinct. Suspicious of institutions, information control, and with some genuine openness around models and tools. All within a markedly nationalist, white framing.

Bottom-right it goes.

In Conclusion: Please Don’t Let the Billionaires Pick the Axes

My exact placements will age badly, maybe even before my flight lands, but the underlying questions will not. Who should AI serve, and who should control it, tells us far more than a left-right label, especially when the people answering those questions also happen to own the models, the data centers, the platforms, and in some cases, a concerning number of rockets.
